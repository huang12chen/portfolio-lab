# Material, Contribution, and Evidence

[简体中文](zh-CN/content.md)

## Extract a useful profile

Work from existing material rather than requiring a new questionnaire: audience and purpose, strengths, projects and their number, responsibilities, media, desired character, publication scope, and technical or maintenance conditions. Existing authorization and preferences remain valid.

Material can include résumés, client deliverables, art, photography, writing, research, repositories, interfaces, operations screenshots, video, workflows, retrospectives, or self-directed practice. Each offers different evidence; a lack of polished images does not make work unworthy of presentation.

## Connect claims to material

Keep important claims traceable. Use the fields relevant to the material; do not invent missing data to fill a schema.

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Project, task, and context | Explain the problem at the time |
| Individual responsibility | Distinguish independent work, collaboration, operations, design, implementation, and use of supplied assets |
| Actual material | File or original link, date, and original version |
| Proposed claim | The concrete statement intended for the page |
| Metric definition | Value, unit, scope, period, and basis |
| Visibility | Public, redacted, local only, or intentionally omitted |
| Limits and unknowns | What the material does not establish |

Do not infer authorship from an asset's presence in a project: organizing product content is different from creating the artwork. Equally, do not reduce substantive responsibility to a vague “assisted” and lose real detail.

## Evidence boundaries

- A count snapshot does not establish growth. Views are not followers, and platform follower counts do not add up to unique people. Preserve a screenshot's measurement window.
- Date historical screenshots. A record saved before leaving a role does not establish an account's current size. If the exact date is unknown, retain a user-confirmed relative time.
- Distinguish revenue or GMV, profit, order-profit rate, gross margin, and net margin. Without attribution evidence, do not credit a specific post with sales.
- Separate workflow descriptions, concept diagrams, real software recordings, and benchmarks. Do not invent time savings without a timing record.
- Attribute client, employer, and team work and describe the person's role. Reuse confirmed usage scope; clarify only an actual unresolved publication restriction and continue other work.
- Preserve originals when redacting. Export a presentation copy and record cropping or masking; do not alter metrics or reconstruct screenshots. Unrelated notifications, birthdays, or recommendations need not appear.
- Synthetic illustrations can explain a concept but cannot substitute for real outcomes. Label concept projects and self-directed practice so they do not resemble client delivery.

For an explicitly authorized fictional teaching portfolio, use the separate [fictional-demo guidance](demo-projects.md). Its invented material must not become evidence for a real person.

## Continue when material is incomplete

Explain which material would strengthen which claim: a listing or account record for operations, a running interface for software, or methods and result figures for research. Organize available evidence and deliver a usable version instead of implying completeness with empty slots.

Publish names, contact details, positioning, and social accounts according to the user's choices. Temporary preview placeholders must be recognizable; a real person's final portfolio must not present invented identity as fact.
