# Adapt Structure to the Work

[简体中文](zh-CN/structure.md)

These are decision starting points, not profession templates. The same field can call for different sites when the material and audience differ.

## Let volume shape access, not the aesthetic

| Available work | Possible organization | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No formal projects | Experience, methods, practice, and process material with honest status; create a real example if useful | Inventing clients or showing planned work as completed |
| One or two strong projects | A substantial single page or a few complete cases, supported by introduction and contact | Duplicating work or empty slots to fill three columns |
| Three to six projects | A comparable overview; categories when differences are meaningful | Hiding the existence of other projects beneath a large first-project banner |
| Seven to twenty projects | Representative cases plus an index based on actual differences | Giving everything equal emphasis or adding empty tags |
| More than twenty assets | Curate by series, capability, or theme; distinguish selected entry points from full collections | Turning every photo into a long case or loading every large image immediately |

A project may contain many assets, and many works may belong to one series. Group first; file count is not project count. Add categories or filters only when they help selection.

## Match evidence to the reader's task

| Field or reader task | Persuasive material | Presentation to explore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design, art, photography, architecture | Originals, series, scale and context, process, individual role | Proportion-aware galleries, series pages, editorial layouts, optional detail zoom |
| Software, product, engineering | Real demos or recordings, code, architecture, constraints, tests, tradeoffs | Running behavior first; interface and implementation near each other; repository as a supporting route |
| Research, education, healthcare, public benefit | Question, method, anonymized material, findings, reflection | Clear reading layouts and figures; minimal sensitive content, no invented documentary photos |
| Writing, content, media | Articles, scripts, excerpts, video, audience-specific expression | Readable excerpts and full-work links, organized by topic or capability |
| Operations, sales, marketing | Listings, content examples, account records, campaign plans, defined outcomes | Evidence and process together; metric summaries for scanning |
| Consulting, services, management | Deliverables, frameworks, process, decisions, team boundaries | Case narratives, redacted excerpts, service capabilities, and contact |
| Career transition or study | Independent work, practice, coursework, transferable skills, reflection | Clear status and evidence of judgment and development; no fabricated commercial experience |

Fields can overlap. Do not automatically choose serious blue or technical black based on a job title. Use the work, audience, materials, and person's character.

## Adapt the case narrative

Select and combine context, contribution, work, method, result, and limits. Visual work may lead with the work itself; a technical case with running behavior; research with the question and finding; writing with a readable excerpt.

Use a concrete project title and an explanatory subtitle where useful. Metrics are not compulsory: supported qualitative outcomes, delivered behavior, and decisions can be sufficient. Do not fill absent results with invented KPIs.

## Contrasting examples, not templates

- An applicant with two anonymized research reports may need a quiet reading homepage, two abstracts, method and figure access, and a contents list for long text, rather than an image carousel.
- A photographer with more than forty differently proportioned images may need curated series, selected work to establish character, and a full-view gallery within each series.
- A developer with four product-engineering projects may need a clear overview, running interfaces beside role and constraints, architecture and tradeoffs in cases, and direct source links.

These examples illustrate reasoning; they do not prescribe a fixed count, layout, or style.

## Choose the reading path before the effect

A gallery index and a continuous story solve different reader tasks. Choose using the actual amount of work, the reader's need to compare, and the effort needed to inspect each artifact.

- A scroll-led page can expose each project's question, individual contribution, core artifact, decision and outcome in one pass. Controls can remain directly usable; further detail is optional.
- An index helps people select among distinct projects or series. Give each a meaningful preview and open the full case directly. Consider a hybrid when a short summary is useful before deeper cases.
- Preserve native scrolling, ordinary links, meaningful anchors and a return route to the place the reader left. Check sticky chapter controls at wide, short and narrow viewports, not only a tall desktop screenshot.

3D, parallax and transitions are presentation choices, not a ranking of portfolio quality. Use them when they express the material or clarify continuity, with readable static content, stable controls, and touch and reduced-motion alternatives. When comparing alternatives, the same content can make their reading and visual differences easier to judge. A comparison is optional, not a requirement to produce two sites for every person.
